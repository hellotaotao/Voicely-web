Transcription apps for iPhone and Mac: where does your audio go?

This page compares the transcription apps you are most likely to find on the App Store, sorted by one question: is your recording turned into text on your own iPhone or Mac, or uploaded to a server first? Voicely, one of the apps listed, is made by us.

Prices and App Store privacy labels checked on the U.S. App Store on November 12, 2026. Transcription providers are quoted from each app's privacy policy.

Two kinds of transcription app

Cloud apps record on your phone and upload the audio. A server, often run by a separate speech-recognition company, turns it into text and sends the text back. The app itself can stay simple, and accuracy depends on whichever speech service the developer pays for. Every minute of audio costs the developer money on that service, so cloud apps limit free minutes and charge by subscription.

On-device apps ship a speech model with the app, or download it once, and run it on the iPhone's Neural Engine or the Mac's GPU. The audio does not have to leave the device, the app works in airplane mode, and another hour of transcription costs the developer nothing. That is why these apps tend to be a one-time purchase or free.

  • A recording usually passes through several companies. Wave's policy names AssemblyAI for transcription, Gladia as a fallback and Google Cloud for storage. Each of them has its own retention rules.
  • Policies are promises. A policy can say recordings are deleted or never used for training, and many are sincere. Once the audio is on someone else's server, though, you have no way to check.

Cloud apps are the right tool for some jobs: a bot that joins your Zoom calls, a shared team workspace, transcripts on a web page for colleagues without the app. If you need those, Otter and its peers do them well.

  • Apple Notes is the zero-setup option: start an audio recording in a note and the transcript appears. Apple's feature availability page lists Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, Korean, French, German, Italian, Portuguese and Spanish alongside English.
  • Whisper Notes is the most complete on-device app today. It labels speakers, lets you pick the model, and costs less than one month of most cloud plans. On Mac it also offers the larger Qwen3-ASR 1.7B model. The trade-off is that notes stay on the device where you recorded them.
  • Aiko is simple and careful: record or drop in a file and get Whisper's transcript. Its listing says it favors accuracy over speed, does not transcribe while recording, and has no speaker detection.
  • Whisper Transcription offers both: a downloadable Whisper model for offline use and a cloud Assistant for speed. If a recording is sensitive, check which one is selected before you transcribe.
  • Voicely picks the models for you and ships them inside the app (about 1.3 GB on iPhone, 1.4 GB on Mac), so there is nothing to download or configure. Auto Detect handles about 30 languages, including meetings where people switch languages mid-sentence. It transcribes while you record, keeps going with the iPhone locked, and syncs notes across iPhone, iPad and Mac through your own iCloud. It has no speaker labels yet and does not run on Intel Macs.

Which one to pick

  • You need a bot in your Zoom calls, or a shared team workspace: a cloud app such as Otter.
  • You need speaker labels today: Whisper Notes.
  • You record now and then and already live in Notes: Apple Notes.
  • You want it free, with the same notes on iPhone and Mac, or your meetings mix languages: Voicely.

Check any app yourself

Turn on airplane mode, switch Wi-Fi off, and transcribe a short recording. A cloud app cannot finish without a connection; an on-device app works the same. Then look at the App Privacy section of its App Store page. Our guide How to tell whether a transcription app uploads your audio goes through four checks step by step.
